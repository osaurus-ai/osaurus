# Agent Channels

Channels (Beta) connect agents to your messaging platforms: Discord, Slack, Telegram, iMessage and WhatsApp, plus Email (IMAP accounts or the Mail app), n8n and custom HTTP channels. Agents can read, draft, and — only with your consent — send messages.

## Setup

- Settings → Channels (in the sidebar's Capabilities group): add a connection per platform with native credential sheets (tokens are saved in General → Vault, never JSON).
- Slack uses a bot token with Socket Mode; Telegram uses a bot via long polling; Discord polls REST (plus Gateway for presence); iMessage uses a local helper downloaded from settings.
- iMessage needs Full Disk Access to read and Messages Automation consent to send.

## The Channels page

- Settings… (⌘,) → Channels lists every connected channel in one table. **By channel** shows, per channel, what it **Receives from** (rooms and senders allowed in), where it **Routes to** (who answers) and what it **Sends** (whether agents may post). **By agent** turns the same table around: for each agent, where it **Answers on** and where it **Posts on its own**, across every channel. Rows are grouped by channel: Discord, Telegram, Slack and Email each have a heading with their accounts under it and an add button (**Add Bot**, **Add Workspace**, **Add Account**). Each row shows one status: a listener problem first, then the first thing setup still needs (*Needs rooms*, *Needs people*, *No one answers*), then whether its listener runs. Click a row to open that account's settings. Platforms with no account yet sit below as tiles.
- **Several accounts**: a channel's add button (or picking it again in **Add Channel**) opens the same setup as its first account, with only **Connect** open: paste the bot token (Slack: bot and app tokens; Email: choose the mail account) and press **Connect** (**Add Account** for Email). The account is checked with the provider, named after the bot, workspace or mail account, and the rest of its setup opens: **Receiving**, **Routing** (who answers), **Sending** and **Test**. Each account is its own channel with its own row, settings, listener, history and routing rules, and shows as "Discord · Support bot" in rules, triggers and history. **Name** at the top of **Connect** renames it. At the bottom of an extra account's **Connect**, **Remove…** removes its settings, token and listener and deletes the triggers on it (the confirmation names them); its stored messages stay. A rule's **Edit…** sheet has an **Account** picker when its channel has several accounts.
- Agents see every account in `agent_channel_list_connections` with its own `connection_id` (`discord-…`, `telegram-…`, `slack-<team>`, `email-…`). Bots and workspaces are also listed under General → Vault → **Accounts**, where an agent can be given the bot it posts as; adding, renaming and removing them happens here (**Manage in Channels**).
- On the first launch after the update, Slack workspaces added before and every Email account past the first become their own accounts: their settings, stored messages and the rules for their rooms move with them (a rule for any room is copied once per account), and a backup is kept in `~/.osaurus/backups/`. Conversations on a moved account start a new chat.

## A channel's sheet: Connect, Receiving, Routing, Sending, Test

- **Connect**: name, bot and tokens. **Receiving**: which rooms and people reach the bot (read allowlist, sender allowlist) plus incoming-message options. **Routing**: who answers. **Sending**: whether the bot may post, the writable rooms, and which agents post on their own. **Test**: a live check.
- Setting a channel up walks these steps with **Back** and **Continue**. A channel that is already set up opens as tabs instead, with **Status** (the same live check, renamed) first and no Back or Continue.
- **Routing → Who answers messages** is a table with one row per rule: **Where** (room chips, or *Any room*), **Who answers** (an agent or a workflow, with badges such as *Starts with “ops”*, *Only when mentioned*, *Filtered*), **Replies** (*Automatically* or *After approval* for agents; *Posts the output*, *Replies from its steps* or *Doesn't reply* for workflows) and an on/off switch. Click the room chips to change rooms; the ⋯ menu has **Edit…** (instructions, aliases, mention gating, thread continuation, filters, queueing), **Duplicate for another room…** and **Delete**.
- **Add Rule** picks the agent or workflow and the rooms it answers in (*Any room*, or *Only these rooms* from the readable rooms; **Add room ID** for one not listed). Many agents across many rooms is just many rows: #support → Triage, #ops → Ops, everything else → Helper. A workflow's required inputs are filled from the message where they can be (an input named after a message field, such as a sender or room, gets that field, and the first other text input gets `{{text}}`); the popover names any required input it could not fill, to bind with **Edit…** after adding.
- **Workflows that reply**: a workflow with a **Reply** step posts into the conversation that started it, mid-run as well as at the end, and can send a file from its run (iMessage and WhatsApp; other channels get the text). A new rule for such a workflow starts on *Replies from its steps* instead of *Posts the output*. With *Posts the output* on as well, the output is posted only when no Reply step posted, and a failed run still says so. A run posts at most 20 replies. The rule's run in **Automations → History** says when a Reply step answered, or why the reply was not posted. `{{caller.channel.room_id}}`, `{{caller.channel.sender_name}}` and the like name the conversation inside the workflow.
- **Forms over the channel**: when a workflow started from a message stops for a form, the bot asks it in the conversation, one question per message ("(1/3) Which project? 1. Alpha 2. Beta"). Only the person who sent the message can answer; reply with the answer, a number for a choice, yes/no, `skip` for an optional question, `keep` to use the shown default, or `cancel` to stop the run. Each answer passes the channel's safety checks like any incoming message, and the form stays open on this Mac as well; whichever answers first wins. In a room where the rule needs a mention (*Only when mentioned*), the answer needs one too. A form that asks for a file, a folder, or a secret is not asked over the channel, nor is one whose sender the platform did not name; the bot says to answer it on the Mac. A question left unanswered for a day, or answered with something that does not fit five times, moves to the Mac with one message saying so. Open questions survive a relaunch: when the run picks up, the bot asks the question it was on, not the whole form again. Each answer shows in **Automations → History** under **Messages** as a *Form answer*.
- How a message is matched: a rule whose alias the message starts with (`ops: restart the build`) wins, then rules for the message's room, then *Any room* rules; every rule in the winning group runs.
- **Where messages go**, under the table, reads the rules back in plain words — "In #support: Triage", "Everywhere else: Helper" — and warns when no rule is on, when a readable room has no rule, when several agents answer the same message, or when a rule's agent is missing.
- Each rule is an automation underneath (its runs are tasks, listed in **Tasks → History**), but channel rules are managed only here; the Automations list points to Channels instead of listing them.
- A message no rule claims stays in the channel's inbox and shows up in **Automations → ⋯ → Channel Messages** with the reason (`no_event_configured`, `no_event_for_room`, `mention_required`, `no_event_matched`). Messages refused by an allowlist show up there too.

## n8n

- Settings → Channels → n8n: a secret-verified webhook (`POST /channels/n8n/{id}/inbound`) with pollable replies. n8n sends an envelope; Osaurus answers with a task id to poll, or pushes the reply to an n8n Webhook trigger.
- n8n connects to Osaurus, not the other way around. The pairing code is the whole hand-off; the only n8n URL Osaurus ever stores is the optional *Outbound Webhook URL* under *Who answers?* for pushing replies.
- The sheet has five steps: *Name it* (display name; the connection id is filled from it), *Where is your n8n?* (This Mac / Docker Desktop / another machine on my network / Remote), *Who answers?* (the **Routing** rules for this connection — **Add Rule** puts an agent or workflow on it — Relay for that agent, optional push), *Pair* (the pairing code), *Prove it* (approve workflows, verify). The on/off switch for a saved channel is on its card in the channel list, not in the sheet.
- *Where is your n8n?* decides everything downstream: the single URL the pairing code carries (`127.0.0.1`, `host.docker.internal`, the LAN address, or the relay URL), whether Relay is required (Remote), whether the connection is end-to-end encrypted, and whether the *Allow plaintext HTTP from other machines* toggle is shown at all (LAN only).
- **Pair with n8n** (in *Pair*) shows one copyable pairing code only once it can work — for Remote that means a local agent answers on this channel and that agent's Relay reports connected; otherwise the card names the blocker and the step that clears it. A new channel gets a random secret automatically (under *Pair → Advanced*, with Rotate). Install the `@osaurus/n8n-nodes-osaurus` community node, paste the code into its **Osaurus Channel** credential and press *Test*. Treat the code like the secret. If you move n8n, change *Where is your n8n?* and copy the code again.
- *Pair → Advanced* holds everything for workflows that use a plain HTTP Request node instead: the inbound and poll URLs for the chosen location, the verification method and header override, sample envelope, HMAC Code node, curl, and the osk-v1 access key for the node's Agent resource.
- *Prove it → Who may speak* is approve-on-first-contact: run the workflow once and it appears as "Workflow *conversation_id* (sender *sender.id*) wants to use *channel* — Allow / Deny". Allow adds those ids to the allowlists and saves immediately; run the workflow again. Until then the node reports `pending_approval` and nothing reaches the agent. Allowlists stay fail-closed and can still be edited by hand under *Advanced*. Hand-typed allowlists do not suppress the prompt: a workflow whose conversation or sender does not match what you typed still shows up under *Who may speak* with Allow / Deny, alongside the existing chips.
- The first run of a new workflow also raises a toast with *Open Channels* when Settings is closed; repeats of the same conversation/sender do not. Pending requests and Deny decisions live only for the current app session — after a relaunch, run the workflow again and it reappears. Approved ids are saved in the allowlists and survive relaunch.
- Channels saved before this flow existed open with *Where is your n8n?* unanswered (unless plaintext was allowed, which only LAN offers). Pick it once; the existing n8n credential keeps working meanwhile, and the next Save stores the choice.

## Email

Email is a channel like the others: mail from allowed senders reaches an agent, and the agent answers by replying in the same thread. Settings → Channels → **Email**. Each Email account watches one mail account with its own settings below; **Add Account** on the Email heading adds another. **Name** (such as "Work mail") starts as the mail account's name.

- **Connect → Choose the mail account** lists your mail accounts; **Watch** the one this Email account reads (one another Email account uses shows "Used by …"), **Edit…** changes it, **Add Account…** adds one:
  - **IMAP server** — Gmail, iCloud, Fastmail or any IMAP server that accepts a password: server, **Security** (TLS, port 993; *None* is only allowed for a server on this Mac, such as Proton Mail Bridge), port, user name and password. With two-step sign-in, use an app password. For replies, add a **Reply Server (SMTP)**, its **Reply Server Security** and **Reply Server Port** (TLS on 465; *None* only for a server on this Mac) and the **From Address**; the reply server signs in with the same user name and password. The password is kept in the Keychain and never exported.
  - **Mail app** — an account already set up in Mail (or all of them); no password, and replies go out through Mail. Use this for Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com, which no longer allow password sign-in over IMAP. Mail must be open for checks to run; the first check asks macOS to let Osaurus control Mail (System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation if it was refused).
  - **Delete Account** (in **Edit…**) removes the account and its password; it is refused while Email still watches it — turn **Watch** off first.
- **Receive Mail** turns checking on for this account.
- **Receiving → Allowed Senders**: one address (`alex@example.com`) or domain (`@example.com`) per line. Mail from anyone else is not kept and never reaches an agent (it shows as refused in **Automations → ⋯ → Channel Messages**); with the list empty, nothing does. Mail from an IMAP account's own address and from automated senders (no-reply, mailer-daemon, postmaster) is ignored.
- For the watched account: **Mailboxes (comma separated; empty = Inbox)** with **Choose…**, **Only Unread Mail**, **Include the Message Text** (cut to **Message Length Limit**; off, agents see only the sender, subject and a short preview), **Check Every** (1–60 minutes) and **Mail That Arrived While Osaurus Was Closed**. **Default Read Limit** (Advanced) is how many messages an agent reads from a mailbox when it does not ask for a number.
- Osaurus only reads mail: it never marks it read, moves or deletes it. Each message reaches agents once (by its Message-ID, remembered across relaunches), even if it shows up again in another mailbox or after a server resync. A newly watched mailbox starts with the next message that arrives. If the server refuses the sign-in (or the password is missing), checks of that account pause so it isn't locked by repeated attempts; editing the account, saving Email or **Check Now** tries again.
- How mail maps onto a channel: the account is the space, each watched mailbox is a room (`<account id>/<mailbox>`, shown as "Work · Inbox"), and each mail thread is one conversation, so follow-ups in a thread land in the same chat.
- **Routing** works as on every channel: **Add Rule** picks the agent or workflow and the mailboxes it answers. A workflow reads the mail as `{{text}}` and `{{email.subject}}`, `{{email.from}}`, `{{email.from_name}}`, `{{email.body}}`, `{{email.snippet}}`, `{{email.attachments}}`.
- **Sending → Allow Sending Email** lets agents reply in the thread of mail they received, from the account it arrived on, to the sender of the latest message (never reply-all). Agents cannot start new email, and the Channels **Allow Agents to Send Messages** switch still applies. Mail app accounts send through Mail; IMAP accounts send through their reply server (an account without one is flagged). SMTP replies use TLS on port 465 (no STARTTLS) and are not copied to the Sent mailbox.
- **Open Unsent Replies as Drafts** (Mail app accounts): when a rule replies *After approval*, the agent's answer opens in Mail as a reply draft for you to review and send.
- **Verify**: the check status of each watched mailbox, recent incoming mail and where it went, **Check Now**, and **Verify incoming message** (send yourself mail from an allowed sender and watch it arrive). **Test Connection** signs in to each watched account.
- Email used to be an automation trigger. Email automations are moved here at launch: each becomes a rule on the Email channel for the same mailboxes and agent or workflow, replying *After approval*; the account's options carry over, its sender filter becomes **Allowed Senders**, and mail already seen is not run again.

## Incoming messages on this Mac

- Each channel conversation is an ordinary chat: the first message opens a tab under the answering agent in the frontmost chat window (without taking focus), and follow-ups on the same conversation land in that same tab. Every run also appears in Activity.
- Settings → Channels → Incoming → **Focus Chat on Incoming Messages** (off by default): when on, every inbound channel message brings its conversation forward — the tab is selected and the chat window comes to the front, opening one if none is up. Leave it off for everyday use; turn it on for a dedicated machine you keep an eye on so each channel activation is visible as it happens.

## Safety model

- Inbound is deny-by-default: only allowlisted spaces/rooms/senders reach an agent; bots and self are ignored unless explicitly allowed.
- Slack stamps messages posted through an app or the API with that app's bot id, even when you post with your own user token, so they are ignored as bot messages. Settings → Channels → Slack → Reply to incoming messages → **Accept Messages Sent Through Apps** (off by default) lets those through when the author is on the sender allowlist, for example a phone shortcut that calls `chat.postMessage`. Any app you authorized to post as you can then reach the agent; other bots stay blocked.
- Writes require the agent to pass `confirm_send: true` and the room to be on a write allowlist; broadcast mentions (@channel and similar) are blocked unless you allow them.
- Channel tools work only inside the app — external HTTP/MCP callers are denied.

## Proactive posting

- Whether an agent may post on its own (not as a reply) is set on the channel: Settings → Channels → the platform → **Sending** → **Agents that post on their own**. **Add Agent** lists an agent; its menu is *Off*, *Asks first* (you see each message before it is sent) or *Without asking* (sends directly; confirm once; use sparingly). The list is available once the bot may post in at least one room (the writable rooms above it); a choice writes one posting place per writable room.
- The agent's **On Channels** list (Agents → the agent → Channels) is read-only — "Slack: answers in #support, replies automatically, posts after asking first" — with **Edit in Channels** on every row. The built-in Default agent cannot answer messages; it can post, and its per-room places are edited with **Edit places…** on its row.
- Modes behind the picker: Off / Draft (writes land in an Outbox for review) / Confirm (asks each time) / Autonomous (sends directly). Draft is only reachable through the Default agent's sheet or `osaurus_config`.
- The Outbox (the **Outbox** button in the Settings → Channels header) holds drafts with mark-sent / retry / discard.

## Diagnostics

Each connection has a diagnostics view (connection state, recent incoming events, and the newest messages in its inbox). Settings → Channels lists connections (Incoming and Sending), with **Outbox** in its header. What happened to each incoming message — ran an automation, refused, or unclaimed — is in **Automations → History** (filter **Messages**, pick the channel; **Copy redacted export** for support). Combine channels with a Schedule automation for recurring digests posted as drafts.

Every outbound delivery — proactive posts (`PUBLISH`) and automatic replies to incoming messages (`REPLY`, with trigger `auto_reply` for agents and `workflow_reply` for a workflow's Reply steps, form questions and final post) — is recorded in Main window → Insights under the **Channels** scope tab: destination, room, size, outcome, and whether attachments were sent. The message text itself is never copied into the log.
